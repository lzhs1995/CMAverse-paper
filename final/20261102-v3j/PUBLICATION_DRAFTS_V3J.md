## 中文标题与摘要草案

**标题：收入变化、婚姻满意度与成年子女对父母的经济支持——传导路径、关系情境及相对评价的区分**

成年子女收入改善是否通过婚姻满意度变化转化为对父母的经济支持，抑或夫妻关系主要构成资源分配的情境条件？本文利用中国家庭追踪调查2018—2020与2020—2022相邻波次的13,092条亲子二元组—区间记录，区分本人和配偶、绝对变化和夫妻内部相对位置变化，结合标准化风险差、加性交互及限定的中介分析进行考察。在本人满意度具提升空间的一个资格样本中，收入增加与支持增长概率高4.90个百分点相关；本人相对满意度位置升级的加性交互为−8.76个百分点，八项BH调整后q为0.032。对1,559条相对位置跃迁记录的拆解显示，28.35%伴随本人评分提高，88.01%伴随配偶评分下降，两者重叠16.36%，说明相对位置升级不能等同于婚姻质量改善。六项附加概率中介规格的逐项未调整区间均含0，现有结果没有提供明确的完整传导证据，但也不能确立等效性或普遍零效应。本文据此强调，夫妻关系的传导作用、情境差异和指标构成需要分别讨论；观察性关联及探索性检验不足以识别特定规范机制或政策收益。

**关键词：**代际经济支持；收入变化；婚姻满意度；夫妻相对评价；统计修饰；中介分析

## English title and abstract draft

**Title: Income Changes, Marital Evaluations, and Adult Children's Financial Support to Parents in China**

How do income changes relate to adult children's financial support to parents, and what role do marital evaluations play? We analyze 13,092 parent–child dyad-period observations from the 2018–2020 and 2020–2022 China Family Panel Studies. Distinguishing mediation from statistical effect modification, we examine respondents and spouses, absolute changes and shifts in within-couple relative positions. In one eligible sample, income increases were associated with a 4.90-percentage-point higher probability of increased parental support. Respondents' relative satisfaction upgrading showed a negative additive interaction of 8.76 percentage points, with a Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted q value of .032 within an eight-test family. Among 1,559 relative-position transitions, 28.35% accompanied an increase in respondents' scores and 88.01% a decrease in spouses' scores, with 16.36% overlapping. Relative upgrading therefore should not be equated with improved marital quality. Six additional probability-scale mediation specifications had individual unadjusted confidence intervals containing zero. These results neither establish equivalence nor demonstrate a universal absence of mediation. The study distinguishes relational context from an established transmission mechanism and clarifies the composition of relative evaluations. Observational measurement, temporal ambiguity, and exploratory specification choices limit causal and policy interpretations; the findings do not directly identify normative rigidity or other unmeasured mechanisms.

**English:**

Our manuscript examines how the association between income changes and upward financial transfers is situated within marital relationships. It separates a proposed transmission pathway from statistical effect modification and distinguishes respondents' and spouses' evaluations, as well as absolute and relative changes. Its contribution is a transparent comparison of evidence levels and a decomposition of relative-position transitions that prevents ranking changes from being mistaken for improved marital quality. The manuscript documents temporal limitations, historical variable construction, exploratory specification choices, and multiple-testing boundaries. It offers evidence relevant to debates about marital relationships and intergenerational resource allocation without claiming identification of unmeasured normative mechanisms.
